The thyroid, a small butterfly-shaped gland in the neck, wields immense influence over our metabolism, energy levels, and overall well-being. When thyroid function becomes imbalanced, it can lead to a range of health issues. Let's delve into the different markers tested in this panel, shedding light on their importance in assessing thyroid function.

#### **Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)**

TSH is often referred to as the "master regulator" of the thyroid. Produced by the pituitary gland (in the brain), it signals the thyroid to produce thyroid hormones, T4 and T3. Elevated TSH levels suggest an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism), while low levels can indicate an overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism).

#### **Free T4**

Free T4, or thyroxine, is a key thyroid hormone that influences metabolism. It's the precursor to the more active hormone, T3. Monitoring free T4 levels helps assess the thyroid's ability to produce this crucial hormone.

#### **Free T3**

Free T3, or triiodothyronine, is the more active thyroid hormone responsible for regulating energy levels, body temperature, and overall cellular function. Monitoring free T3 levels provides insights into cellular metabolism.

#### **Reverse T3**

Reverse T3 is an inactive form of T3. Elevated levels may indicate a physiological response to stress, leading the body to produce more reverse T3 than active T3. This can hinder metabolism and energy production.

#### **TPO Antibodies**

Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO) antibodies indicate an autoimmune response targeting the thyroid. Elevated levels suggest Hashimoto's thyroiditis, an autoimmune condition causing hypothyroidism.

#### **Thyroglobulin Antibodies**

Thyroglobulin antibodies are another marker of autoimmune thyroid conditions. Elevated levels can indicate Hashimoto's or Graves' disease, an autoimmune disorder causing hyperthyroidism.

#### **Thyroglobulin**

Thyroglobulin is a protein the thyroid uses to produce thyroid hormones. Elevated levels can indicate thyroid inflammation, enlargement or cancer.
